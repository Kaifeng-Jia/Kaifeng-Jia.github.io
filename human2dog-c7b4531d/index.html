A person pushes a box (left). B2-Z1 pushes it at the same contact point, at the same time, while walking (right).

Human2Dog Retarget transfers human–object interactions from the OMOMO dataset to quadrupeds with arms. The robot touches the object where and when the person did, and the object moves the same way.

Abstract

People pushing boxes, dragging tables and lifting trash cans are easy to record. A quadruped with an arm has a different body, so copying the person's joint angles does not work. We retarget OMOMO human–object interactions to quadruped manipulators and keep what defines the interaction: where and when the gripper touches the object, and how the object moves.

For every frame, a trust-region SQP maps the human–object interaction network onto the robot. Its terms are ranked: collision, then contact, then gait, then relation. Without gait design, the solver places the body and arm and lets the feet slide. With gait design, it adds a lateral-sequence walk: stance feet do not slip, and swing feet follow planned trajectories. Robot sizes come from the robot descriptions, so the same code retargets to four quadruped manipulators: B2-Z1, Go2-ARX5, Go2-PiPER and Spot-Arm.

We then train whole-body tracking policies in Isaac Lab on the retargeted clips. Plain motion tracking learns a shortcut: the robot pushes the box with its body and holds the arm up. Residual actions, a contact reward and a lost-contact termination, taken from HDMI, make the gripper push at the contact point. The RL stage is work in progress.

Method

Three parts: an interaction network says what to keep, a ranked optimization solves each frame, and a gait design makes the feet step.

1Interaction network

Each point on the person has a counterpart on the robot: pelvis to trunk centre, elbow to arm elbow, hand to gripper, and the four foot points to the support centre. Two more points ride on the person's trunk and stand for the robot's trunk front and arm base. The retargeter keeps the graph's edges, not the joint angles, so the robot stands in the same relation to the object as the person.

2Per-frame optimization

Each frame is a trust-region SQP. Its terms are ranked: no collision first, then the gripper on the contact point, then the gait, then the relation edges.

3Gait design

B2-Z1 walks with a lateral-sequence walk: left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore. Its stride frequency follows the person's walking speed, scaled to the robot's leg length by dynamic similarity, and each swing foot lands at a Raibert foothold.
